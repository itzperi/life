## #2 Guide refresh (Wed 18 Nov 2026)

Publish as a new top section of `/guides/contract-vs-permanent-vs-payroll-staffing/`.

**Title:** Labour Codes, one year on: a checklist for companies that use contract staff

**Meta description:** The Labour Codes took effect on 21 Nov 2025. A plain checklist for Tamil Nadu employers using contract staff: licences, PF, ESI and records.

India's four Labour Codes took effect on 21 November 2025. A year later, many employers in Tamil Nadu still manage contract staff the old way. This checklist covers what to ask your manpower agency, and what to keep in your own files. Figures are as of Nov 2026. State rules vary, so verify before you act. This is general information, not legal advice.

## What changed when the Labour Codes took effect?

Four Labour Codes replaced 29 older central labour laws on 21 November 2025. They cover wages, industrial relations, social security, and occupational safety and health. For contract staffing, the big change is in the OSH Code: the contract labour licence threshold moved from 20 to 50 contract workers. State rules still vary.

The four codes are the Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code, the Code on Social Security and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code. Each state also issues its own rules under them. Read the Tamil Nadu rules that apply to your type of unit. Sources: treelife.in and india-briefing.com summaries, as of Nov 2026.

## Does my manpower agency need a contract labour licence?

Under the OSH Code, a contractor employing 50 or more contract workers needs a licence. Below 50, the licence rule may not apply, but state rules can differ. Ask your agency in writing how many contract workers it employs in total, and which licence or registration it holds. Keep a copy on file.

Also check the agency's GST registration and its PF and ESI codes. A genuine agency will share these without fuss. Lifeline Placements' licences and registrations: [licences to confirm].

## Who is responsible if the contractor does not pay PF or ESI?

The contractor deducts and pays PF and ESI for its workers. But the principal employer, the company where the staff work, can be held liable if the contractor fails to pay. So check proof of payment every month, not just at the start. Ask for challans and the wage register.

The rates, as of Nov 2026:
- **ESI:** employer 3.25%, employee 0.75%, for wages up to Rs 21,000 a month. Source: ESIC.
- **EPF:** 12% from the employer and 12% from the employee. Several sources report the EPF wage ceiling rose from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 on 17 September 2026 (S.O. 5109(E)). Confirm with EPFO before you budget.

## What should I keep in my own files each month?

Keep five things each month: the attendance sheet you signed, the agency's invoice, the PF and ESI challans for your staff, the wage register or payslips, and proof that wages reached workers' bank accounts. If an inspector or a worker raises a question, these papers answer it quickly.

A simple folder per month works. Some employers ask the agency to email all five items by a fixed date, such as the 15th. Put that date in the agreement.

## Should I move from contract to permanent or payroll staffing?

Not just because the law changed. Contract staffing still suits seasonal or project work. Permanent placement suits roles you will keep for years. Payroll staffing suits people you choose yourself but want someone else to pay and file for. Match the model to how long you need the role.

A quick way to decide:
- Need staff for a season, a new line or a project? **Contract staffing.**
- Hiring for a role you will keep for years? **Permanent placement.** You pay a one-time fee and the person joins your rolls.
- Already found the person, but do not want them on your rolls yet? **Payroll staffing.** Whether Lifeline offers payroll staffing: [payroll service to confirm].

**Title:** Bulk hiring checklist: what to fix before you ask for 50 or more workers

**Meta description:** A practical checklist for Tamil Nadu employers hiring 50 or more workers: job details, joining batches, day-one documents, PF and ESI.

Bulk hiring goes wrong for simple reasons. The job is not written down. The wage changes after interviews. Fifty people arrive on one day and there are uniforms for twenty. This checklist fixes those problems before they start. It suits factory lines, warehouses, e-commerce peaks, garment units and large facility contracts.

## What should be ready before you ask an agency for 50 or more workers?

Five things: a written job description for each role, the wage and shift for each, a joining date, a site contact who can approve candidates, and day-one arrangements such as uniforms, ID cards, safety gear and canteen passes. Missing any one of these slows the whole batch.

Write the job description in plain words. "Packer, standing work, 8-hour shift, lifting up to 15 kg" helps us screen. "Helper" does not. Say whether Tamil, Hindi or English is needed on the floor. Say if the role needs ITI, a diploma, or a forklift licence.

## How much notice does an agency need for bulk hiring?

It depends on the role, the number, the location and the shift. Helpers in the Chennai belt are usually quicker to fill than skilled operators at a distant unit. Lifeline's planning time for bulk orders is 48 hours for helpers and 7 days for skilled roles. Give the agency your date early, even if the numbers may change.

Share a rough forecast as soon as you have one. "Around 60 people from the second week of January" is enough to start sourcing. Confirm the final number a week before joining. The largest single order Lifeline supports: [capacity to confirm].

## Should all workers join on the same day?

Usually not. Joining in batches of 10 to 20 lets your supervisors train properly and spot problems early. It also gives the agency time to replace anyone who does not report. Plan the batches with your line or shift heads, not only with HR.

A simple plan: batch 1 on day 1, batch 2 on day 3, batch 3 on day 5. Hold a 30-minute induction for each batch: safety, canteen, transport, attendance and who to call.

## What documents will workers need on day one?

Usually an identity proof, an address proof, bank account details, passport-size photos and, where relevant, education or skill certificates. For contract staff, the agency also needs details to register each worker for PF and ESI. Agree the list in advance so nobody is turned away at the gate.

Send the list to the agency in writing. Ask the agency to collect and check documents before joining day. Keep copies secure and share them only with people who need them.

## How do you reduce drop-offs in the first week?

Tell candidates the full truth before they join: wage in hand, shift, location, transport, food and overtime rules. Many early exits come from surprises. Then check in on day 1, day 3 and day 7. A short call from the supervisor or the agency catches problems while they can still be fixed.

Track three numbers per batch: how many were selected, how many joined, and how many were still working on day 7. Share them with the agency every week. Patterns show up quickly, such as a shift that nobody wants or a bus route that does not work.

## Who handles PF, ESI and wages in bulk contract hiring?

In contract staffing, the agency is the immediate employer. It pays wages and deposits PF and ESI. Your company, as the principal employer, should still check proof every month. As of Nov 2026, ESI covers wages up to Rs 21,000 a month (source: ESIC). Lifeline's compliance scope is [to confirm].

**Title:** What does staffing cost in Tamil Nadu? Wages, ESI, EPF, agency fees and GST

**Meta description:** How a contract staffing quote is built in Tamil Nadu: gross wage, ESI and EPF (as of Nov 2026), agency fee ranges, GST and a worked illustration.

Two staffing quotes for the same role can differ by a large amount. Usually the difference is not the agency's fee. It is what each quote includes. This guide shows how a monthly cost is built, so you can compare quotes line by line. All statutory figures are as of Nov 2026, with sources. Verify them before you budget. This is general information, not tax or legal advice.

## What makes up the monthly cost of one contract worker?

Four parts: the worker's gross wage, the employer's statutory contributions such as ESI and EPF, the agency's service charge, and GST. Some quotes also add bonus, leave, uniform or transport costs. Ask every agency for a line-by-line breakup, so you can compare quotes fairly.

A quote that shows only one "all-in" figure per head is hard to check. Ask for the gross wage, each statutory line, the service charge and GST, shown separately.

## What are the ESI and EPF rates as of Nov 2026?

As of Nov 2026, ESI is 3.25% from the employer and 0.75% from the employee, for wages up to Rs 21,000 a month. EPF is 12% from each side. Several sources report the EPF wage ceiling rose from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 on 17 September 2026. Confirm with EPFO.

Sources: ESIC for ESI rates; EPFO notification S.O. 5109(E) as reported, for the EPF ceiling. EPFO also levies small administration and insurance (EDLI) charges on the employer. Ask the agency to show these as separate lines.

## What is the minimum wage in Tamil Nadu?

Tamil Nadu sets minimum wages by type of work and by zone. It revised them from 1 April 2026, under a notification dated 18 February 2026. Each rate has a basic part and a variable dearness allowance (VDA). Check the exact rate for your type of work with the TN Labour Department before you budget.

Yes. Vendors quote 18% GST on staffing services. Whether it is calculated only on the service charge or on the full invoice depends on how the contract and invoice are structured. Ask your chartered accountant before comparing quotes, because this one line can change the total noticeably.

**Title:** Replacement guarantees in staffing: what to ask and what is normal

**Meta description:** What a staffing replacement guarantee covers, common replacement windows, usual exclusions and what to put in the agreement. For Tamil Nadu employers.

A new joiner leaves in week three. Will the agency send someone else? At what cost? How fast? The answers sit in the replacement clause. Many employers sign without reading it. This guide explains what to ask, and what is common in the market.

## What is a replacement guarantee in staffing?

It is the agency's promise to find another candidate, at no extra fee, if a placed person leaves or is let go within a fixed period. The terms decide which exits count, how fast the replacement comes, and whether any refund applies. Read them before you sign.

The guarantee matters most for permanent placement, where you pay a one-time fee. In contract staffing, the worker stays on the agency's rolls, so replacement is usually part of the ongoing service.

## What replacement period is normal?

Vendors commonly publish replacement windows of 30 to 90 days for permanent placement. Longer windows usually come with a higher fee or conditions. For contract staffing, replacement is often ongoing for the contract period. Lifeline's terms are [replacement terms to confirm]. Ask us for them in writing.

The 30 to 90 day range comes from published vendor terms (as of Nov 2026). It is not a legal rule. A longer window is not always better if it comes with many exclusions.

## Which exits are usually not covered?

Many agreements exclude exits caused by the employer: changing the job, cutting the agreed salary, changing shifts without notice, or closing the position. Some also exclude terminations unrelated to performance. Ask the agency to list the exclusions in plain words, so there is no argument later.

Common exclusions to look for:
- Salary or role changed after joining
- Position closed or put on hold
- Layoffs or restructuring
- Fee not paid on time
- Employer hired the candidate through another route

None of these is unfair in itself. The problem is finding out after the exit.

## How fast should a replacement arrive?

It depends on the role. A helper or packer is usually quicker to replace than a qualified technician. Agree a target in writing, such as a number of working days, and agree what happens if the agency misses it. Lifeline's replacement timeline is [turnaround to confirm].

For bulk and contract work, also agree how absentees are covered day to day. For example, can the agency send a stand-in for a worker on leave? Write that down too.

## What should the agreement say about replacement?

At minimum: the replacement period, which exits are covered, how many replacements per position, the time to replace, whether a refund or credit applies if no replacement is found, and how you must notify the agency. Put it in the agreement itself, not only in an email.

A simple clause template to discuss with the agency:
- Replacement period: [__] days from the joining date
- Replacements per position: [__]
- Time to replace: [__] working days from written notice

- Notice: by email or WhatsApp to the named account manager

## What can you do to make placements last?

Share the real job before interviews: wage in hand, shift, location, duties and growth. Give each new joiner a named supervisor and a first-week plan. Tell the agency early if something is going wrong. A replacement clause is a safety net, not a substitute for good onboarding.

A day-7 call with the new joiner, by the agency or your supervisor, catches most problems while they can still be fixed. Ask the agency whether it makes this call.
